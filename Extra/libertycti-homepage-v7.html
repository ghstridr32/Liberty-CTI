Lou Maldonado
Intelligence Officer  ·  Russian Linguist  ·  Cyber & Geopolitical Threat Analysis  ·  USAF (Ret.)

Born in the Bronx, Lou Maldonado enlisted as a Russian linguist in 1997 and built a 30‑year intelligence career spanning the NSA, the White House Situation Room, the Pentagon, and the National Security Council. He ultimately commanded the NSA Cybersecurity Threat Operations Center–Texas and a 270‑person cyber intelligence squadron at Joint Base San Antonio–Lackland.

His analytical work has focused on nation‑state cyber operations, strategic competition, and the threat landscape facing critical infrastructure and defense industries. This includes intelligence and policy work related to Russia, Ukraine, strategic arms control, and the broader geopolitical drivers of cyber conflict.

  • Duty Officer, White House Situation Room — National Security Council
  • Director for Legislative Affairs, National Security Council — responsible for congressional engagement on Russia, Ukraine, and strategic arms control policy
  • Commander, 35th Intelligence Squadron — NSA Cybersecurity Threat Operations Center–Texas
  • Commanded 270‑person cyber intelligence squadron, JB San Antonio–Lackland
  • Chief, C4ISR Programs — Legislative Liaison, Office of the Secretary of the Air Force
  • Director of Intelligence, Combined Joint Special Operations Aviation Component–Afghanistan
  • ISR Mission Commander across Electronic Warfare, Cyber & Special Operations units

Co-Founder & Senior Intelligence Analyst
Angie Maldonado
Intelligence Officer  ·  Russian Language Professional  ·  USAF (Ret.)

Raised in Southern California, Angie Maldonado enlisted as a Russian linguist in August 1997 — the same year as Lou, the same language, the same path. She commissioned through Officer Training School on October 27, 2009 — the same date as Lou. Over three decades she built one of the most diverse intelligence portfolios in the Air Force: targeting, ISR strategy, culture and language intelligence, special operations, and ultimately serving as Junior Military Assistant to the Deputy Secretary of Defense. Most recently she commanded the 324th Training Squadron at Joint Base San Antonio–Lackland, leading 75 personnel and producing over 5,000 warfighters annually for the operational Air Force.

Adversary Landscape

We Track the Actors
Targeting Your Sector

Liberty CTI monitors six primary threat ecosystems with sustained targeting interest in Texas critical infrastructure and the broader U.S. economy.

Nation-State · PRC
People's Republic of China

PRC cyber espionage operations targeting the Texas defense industrial base, technology sector, and critical infrastructure represent the most sustained long-term threat to U.S. economic and national security interests.

Nation-State · Russia
Russian Federation

Russian state actors and criminal ecosystems operating with Kremlin tolerance pose persistent threats to Texas energy infrastructure, financial systems, and government networks — tracked by our Russian-fluent analyst team.

Nation-State · Iran
Islamic Republic of Iran

IRGC-affiliated cyber units demonstrate consistent interest in U.S. energy infrastructure and financial systems, with operations intensifying during periods of geopolitical escalation and sanctions pressure.

Nation-State · DPRK
North Korea

DPRK cyber operations targeting financial institutions and cryptocurrency infrastructure fund the regime's weapons programs, with Texas financial sector firms representing high-value targets.

Criminal Ecosystem
Ransomware Syndicates

Russian-nexus ransomware organizations are the primary operational threat to Texas healthcare systems, municipal government, and mid-market energy sector companies operating below enterprise security thresholds.

Emerging Threat
Hacktivist Proxy Networks

Nation-state-enabled hacktivist networks provide plausible deniability for state actors conducting disruptive operations against critical infrastructure and financial targets aligned with geopolitical objectives.
