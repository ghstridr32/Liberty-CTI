Executive Warning: The exploited seam this week is not a network perimeter — it is the boundary between digital access and operational authority, and adversaries are working both sides of it. A water-sector intrusion campaign first confirmed in Minnesota has widened to Michigan and New Jersey, with unconfirmed wire reporting placing the total at twelve states; attackers who gain remote control of small-utility systems are forcing operators into manual operations to preserve safety. Separately, an identity-centric extortion group has pivoted from retail and healthcare into private equity, hedge funds, and law firms — walking through corporate identity systems by phone call rather than by exploit. Neither campaign has produced a confirmed Texas-specific incident, but both expose a pattern Texas leadership should not assume applies only to someone else’s utility or someone else’s bank. A federal remediation deadline and a state grid-connection audit both landed this week, compressing the window boards have to decide who holds emergency authority before the next event forces the question.

Board Implications This Week — Ask

Who is authorized, right now, to isolate OT remote access or lock down an identity/help-desk workflow during an active campaign — without waiting for a scheduled meeting?

Board Implications This Week — Direct

Require a named executive for OT-isolation authority and a named executive for identity/help-desk lockdown authority, confirmed in writing this week.

Board Implications This Week — Document

Record which vulnerability-remediation exceptions leadership has accepted this week and why, ahead of the 14 August federal remediation-policy milestone.

Board Implications This Week — Rehearse

Run a five-minute tabletop: your help desk gets a call from someone with an employee’s real phone number, capable of capturing their MFA token live. Who do they call, and what can that person authorize without you?

Dominant Geo-Cyber Judgment

Liberty CTI assesses with high confidence that the exploited failure point across this week’s two lead stories is the same: control over operational or identity systems is shifting to whoever can reach the access channel, not whoever holds the perimeter. Water utilities are losing control of programmable control systems through internet-exposed remote-access paths. Financial and legal firms are losing control of identity through help-desk trust, not through defeated multi-factor authentication. These are different sectors and different adversary types, but they present the same executive decision problem: who has pre-authorized emergency authority to sever or restrict an access channel before an intrusion becomes an operational or extortion event.

This is an escalation of a standing call, not a new hypothesis. ATB-2026-21 assessed the water-sector campaign’s occurrence at high confidence — confirmed operational effects, seven-plus states — with Iranian affiliation carried as the leading hypothesis at moderate confidence, explicitly unconfirmed. This week’s reporting is consistent with that trend line and does not change either judgment. What is genuinely new this week is the identity-centric extortion campaign against major financial and private-equity firms, which has not previously received standalone ATB treatment.

Bottom line: Attribution on the water campaign remains unresolved — treat it as assessed likely origin, not confirmed fact, in any board or regulator communication. The finance-sector campaign requires no attribution debate to be actionable: the technique defeats trust, not technology, and any organization running federated identity with a human-staffed help desk is a plausible target regardless of sector label.

Section I

What Changed This Week

Water-Sector Campaign Widens to Michigan and New Jersey

Michigan confirmed cyber incidents affecting nine water systems, and New Jersey confirmed two affected utilities where operators shifted to manual operations without service disruption or contamination. Unconfirmed wire reporting (Axios, ABC News, anonymous sourcing) places the total number of affected states at twelve as of 4 August, alongside a separate finding that a volunteer cyber-defense corps has reached only 21 of roughly 50,000 small water utilities nationwide. Liberty CTI treats the twelve-state figure as directionally consistent with the confirmed trend but not yet corroborated to the standard of the named-official confirmations in Michigan and New Jersey.

Identity-Centric Extortion Pivots Into Major Financial and Private-Equity Firms

An extortion cluster tracked by primary vendor research rebranded from a retired public identity into several new fronts and shifted its primary targeting from retail and healthcare into private equity, hedge funds, law firms, and ratings agencies. Reporting names several large financial and private-equity firms among those targeted this month; some have confirmed attempted or blocked intrusions, most declined further comment. The technique bypasses multi-factor authentication by convincing an employee to authenticate the attacker, not by defeating the control technically. See Technical Annex for full TTP detail.

Texas Orders Audit of Entire Data-Center Interconnection Queue

On 3 August, Governor Abbott directed PUCT and ERCOT to audit every data-center project in the grid interconnection queue — roughly 1,800 projects, over 474 GW, approximately 90 percent data centers — before any further connections proceed. ERCOT has paused its Batch Zero classification notifications and will seek a good-cause exception at PUCT’s 20 August open meeting. This is a governance and timeline development, not a cyber incident, but it directly affects Energy and AI/cloud clients with projects in the queue and compounds the OT-exposure question raised by the water-sector campaign: audited interconnection means audited infrastructure.

CMMC Reform Window Closes in One Week

The Department of War’s CMMC Reform Task Force Request for Information closes 14 August, with recommendations due to the CMMC Reform Task Force by mid-September. Phase I self-assessment, DFARS 252.204-7012, and False Claims Act exposure remain fully in force regardless of the Phase II suspension. Defense contractors have one week left to shape the reformed program before the input window closes.

Section III

Geopolitical Driver

The strategic backdrop remains Iranian OT targeting tied to retaliation capacity amid continued U.S.-Iran friction, layered onto a persistent baseline of criminal extortion ecosystems monetizing identity trust rather than technical exploits. What changed this week is not the underlying driver — it is the demonstrated reach of both patterns: OT compromise expanding geographically among under-resourced utilities, and identity compromise expanding upmarket into sophisticated, well-defended financial institutions.

Continuum assessment: Late-stage Competition with acute Crisis-conditioning indicators, unchanged from ATB-2026-21. No retained reporting supports a transition to Conflict. No Flash Warning threshold was met.

Section IV

Why This Matters to Texas

No Texas utility and no Texas-domiciled financial or legal firm has been named in either campaign. That is not the same as Texas being unexposed. The confirmed water-sector technique does not require a sophisticated or well-resourced target, and Texas has hundreds of small municipal water systems with comparable remote-access architecture. The confirmed finance-sector technique does not require a technical vulnerability at all — only a help desk willing to trust a caller. Texas energy trading desks, PE-backed operators, and law firms serving them carry the same exposure as their Wall Street counterparts.

Cascade risk: A successful intrusion creates extortion, disclosure, privilege, and customer-trust decisions even when core systems remain fully operational.

Cascade risk: Healthcare degradation creates immediate safety pressure; DIB degradation creates contract-performance and compliance pressure ahead of a hard deadline.

Governance Implication Block

Consequence of delay
An intrusion that could have been contained in hours becomes a multi-day manual-operations event, an extortion negotiation, or a disclosure decision made for the first time under live pressure — with no prior record showing informed oversight.

Confirm, in writing, who can order OT isolation/manual operations and who can suspend help-desk/identity workflows during an active campaign. Run the five-minute help-desk tabletop described above.
Record to preserve
The named authority for OT isolation and identity lockdown, the date it was confirmed, any vulnerability-remediation exceptions accepted this week and why, and documentation of ERCOT/PUCT interconnection exposure for any client with projects in the queue.
